# Memory benchmark suite (Phase 3c)

Issue #8, part of the campaign in issue #2 (sections 7 and 9). This note defines every
benchmark task, its exact optimum, its known memory requirements, where each definition
comes from, and what we had to assume. The generated tables are in
[`results/summaries/phase3c/suite.md`](../results/summaries/phase3c/suite.md) (exact
figures and literature checks) and
[`results/summaries/phase3c/sanity.md`](../results/summaries/phase3c/sanity.md) (sanity
runs of architecture A). Both come from one command:

```sh
uv run python experiments/phase3c.py --config experiments/configs/phase3c.toml
```

## 1. How to use it

Every task is built with `qmem.tasks.make_task({"kind": ..., **params})` and has the
`HMMTask` interface from Phase 2: `sample(n, length, seed)`, `predictive`, `log_probs`
(the source's own per-symbol log-probabilities, so the paired *gap* model − source is
exact on any test set), `step_entropies`, `optimal_nll(T)`, `entropy_rate()` and
`describe()`. Splits come from `qmem.data.make_splits` with recorded seeds, as in Phase 2.

| module | what it adds |
|---|---|
| `qmem.tasks` | the task constructors below; `QuantumSourceTask` (sources generated by a `qmem.Instrument`); `channel_fixed_point`; `pad_alphabet` |
| `qmem.complexity` | `epsilon_machine` (causal states, `C_mu`), `quantum_overlaps` (`C_q(L)`, `C_q`, quantum dimension), `memory_profile`, and two exact quantum models: `qmachine_instrument` (any unifilar source) and `pmd_qubit_instrument` (Wu et al.'s 1-qubit PMD model) |
| `qmem.benchmarks` | `LITERATURE` (every number taken from a paper, with its source and our recomputation), `literature_checks`, `describe_benchmark`, `recall_metrics`, `description_accuracy` |
| `qmem.diagnostics` | stationary memory and its entropy, retention curves, reference-qubit purification, for any instrument (see section 5) |

**Alphabets.** Architecture A emits `2**n_output` symbols. Sources with 3 or 6 symbols
(Ghafari, Stern–Gerlach, the 6-state HMM) are padded with never-emitted symbols to 4 or
8 (`n_symbols` parameter; `n_symbols = None` gives the native alphabet). A model must
then also learn to give the padding symbols probability 0. The suite table lists the
emitted count in brackets.

**Optima.** All sources here except two are *unifilar* (state and symbol fix the next
state), so `optimal_nll(T) = H(Y_1..Y_T)/T` is exact by belief enumeration at any `T`
and the entropy rate is exact (`sum_i pi_i H(Y | i)`). The two quantum sources (clock,
Stern–Gerlach) are exact too: their conditional states take finitely many values per
step, and their rate is the limit of the step entropies (to 1e-13). Two sources are
non-unifilar, and their exact optimum at length 32 cannot be enumerated: the 6-state
HMM and the hidden variant of mixed timescales. For them, `evaluate` records the
optimum as NaN with a note, the paired gap to the source (which uses the exact
per-sequence `log_probs`) stays exact, and the suite reports a Monte Carlo entropy rate
with its SE.

**Memory figures** (bits). For unifilar sources `qmem.complexity` computes:

- the **causal states** and the **statistical complexity** `C_mu = H(pi)`, by merging
  predictively equivalent states (partition refinement), and the topological complexity
  `D_mu = log2(#states)`;
- the **quantum causal states** of the standard phaseless unitary simulator
  ([Binder2018] Eq. 5; [Gu2012]; [Mahoney2016]). Their overlaps solve
  `<s_j|s_k> = sum_x sqrt(P(x|j)P(x|k)) <s_λ(j,x)|s_λ(k,x)>`. Iterating from the identity
  gives exactly the length-`L` q-machine of [Mahoney2016] after `L` iterations, so one
  loop yields `C_q(L)`, its limit `C_q = S(sum_j pi_j |s_j><s_j|)`, and the **quantum
  dimension** (the rank of the overlap matrix). `tests/test_benchmarks.py` checks
  `C_q(L)` against explicitly built q-machine states for `L` ≤ 4.

Phase-enhanced encodings [Liu2019] can need less memory than the phaseless one. The PMD
processes are the case that matters here: phaseless, they need `N` (or `N − 1`)
dimensions, while Wu et al.'s construction with phases needs 2 (section 2.3). Both are
reported.

**Independent checks.** `qmachine_instrument` turns the quantum causal states into an
explicit instrument (`K_x = (<x| ⊗ I) M` with `M` the isometry
`|s_j> -> sum_x sqrt(P(x|j)) |x>|s_λ(j,x)>`). Running it as a `QuantumSourceTask`
reproduces each source's predictive distribution to 1e-9, so each claimed quantum memory
dimension is backed by a working model of that size. The same holds for Wu et al.'s
1-qubit PMD model, which matches to 1e-15.

### 2.1 Perturbed coin [Gu2012]

A coin flips with probability `p` each step and is then observed. There are two causal
states (the last symbol), so `C_mu = 1` bit for `p ≠ 1/2`. The quantum causal states are
`sqrt(1−p)|0> + sqrt(p)|1>` and `sqrt(p)|0> + sqrt(1−p)|1>`, giving
`C_q = H(1/2 + sqrt(p(1−p)))`.

- **Reproduced:** the closed form, which matches the overlap computation to 1e-12, and
  both thresholds in the text: `C_q < 0.5` for p > 0.2 (ours 0.469 at 0.2) and
  `C_q < 0.1` for p > 0.4 (ours 0.081 at 0.4).
- **Assumption:** the paper writes `C_q` with `ln` in one place and `log` elsewhere. We
  use bits. Both thresholds hold in either base.

### 2.2 Ghafari / asymmetric process [Ghafari2019PRX; Yang2021]

This is a perturbed coin with flip probabilities `p` (0→1) and `q` (1→0), post-processed
so that every 0 that precedes a 1 is written as 2. The causal state is the last output.
The transitions are read off Ghafari Fig. 1b, and Yang Fig. 3a draws the identical
machine (Yang's "simple asymmetric process"):

`S0 −0|1−p→ S0`, `S0 −2|p→ S2`, `S1 −1|1−q→ S1`, `S1 −0|q(1−p)→ S0`, `S1 −2|pq→ S2`,
`S2 −1|1→ S1`.

The process has three classical states, and its quantum causal states span 2 dimensions
for every `(p, q)`. Their overlaps `√q`, `√(1−q)` and 0 match Ghafari Eq. 2. This is the
dimensional-advantage test: an exact 1-qubit model exists (we build it with
`qmachine_instrument`), while no 2-state classical model is exact.

- **Reproduced:** 3 causal states; quantum dimension 2; `C_mu = 1.5` and
  `C_q = 0.811` at `p = q = 0.5`. Ghafari Fig. 4b, read off the plot, shows a peak of
  1.5 and a minimum of about 0.82.
- **Assumptions:** the alphabet {0, 1, 2} is padded to 4 symbols. The suite uses
  `p = q = 0.5` (Ghafari's `p = q` cut, where `C_mu` peaks) and `p = 0.3, q = 0.8` as a
  second point (`ghafari_p03_q08`). Yang et al.'s own parameter values appear only in figures that we could
  not read reliably, so that point is illustrative, not theirs.
- **Not reproduced here:** Yang et al.'s *provable bound* on the best 2-state classical
  model's distortion. "Beats any 2-state classical model" needs that bound, which belongs
  with the classical baselines (#6) and the comparison grid.

### 2.3 PMD renewal processes [Wu2023]

The survival function is `Φ(n) = Γ^n (1 − V sin²(nπ/N))`, the probability that
consecutive 1s are separated by at least `n` 0s. We use `P(0 | n zeros) = Φ(n+1)/Φ(n)`
(Wu Methods, Eq. 4). The hazard is `N`-periodic, so the counter mod `N` is the causal
state: there are `N` classical states and `D_mu = log2 N`. The constructor rejects
parameters with a non-decreasing `Φ` (Wu, footnote 2). The suite uses Wu's Fig. 5a
setting, `Γ = 0.5` and `V = 0.4`, for N = 3..8. The experiment used `Γ` ∈ [0.49, 0.64].

- **Reproduced:** `N` causal states for N = 3..8. Wu's main theory result, an exact
  model with one qubit for every PMD process, is implemented from Supplement S1
  (`pmd_qubit_instrument`). `ηA_0` has eigenvalues `1, e^{iφ}` with `φ = 2π/N`;
  `A_1 = ζ|σ_0><v_1|`; `η` and `α` come from Eq. S10, and `β_0, β_1` from Eq. S12 with
  `V = 4αβ_0β_1`. Its survival function equals `Φ(n)` to 1e-12, and its predictive
  distribution equals the classical PMD predictive to 1e-15, for every N = 3..8 and
  `Γ` ∈ {0.49, 0.57, 0.64} (tests).
- **Finding worth noting:** the *phaseless* quantum causal states of PMD processes span
  `N` dimensions (`N − 1` for N = 6, 8), so the 1-qubit compression depends on the
  complex phases `e^{inφ}` in Wu's encoding. For a learned model this means a 1-qubit
  instrument can be exact for every N, but only with complex amplitudes.
- **Not reproduced:** the experiment's per-N KL divergences (< 1e-2 bits). They measure
  the photonic hardware, not the process.

### 2.4 Upset gambler [Binder2018]

The process has two causal states, A and B: `P(0, B | A) = p`, `P(1, A | A) = 1 − p`,
`P(0, A | B) = q`, `P(1, A | B) = 1 − q`, with `p < q`. It has infinite Markov order: a
run of 0s alternates between the states.

- **Reproduced:** `C_mu ≈ 0.98` and `C_q ≈ 0.10` at p = 0.7, q = 0.8 (Fig. 2 caption;
  ours 0.9774 and 0.0996). The overlap formula of Eq. 13,
  `<s_A|s_B> = sqrt((1−p)(1−q)) / (1 − sqrt(pq))`, matches to 1e-10.

### 2.5 Uniform renewal [Yang2021; Wang2026]

The number of 0s between consecutive 1s is uniform on `0..N−1`, so the hazard after `k`
zeros is `1/(N − k)` and there are `N` causal states. Both papers state this definition
in their text and figure captions. Yang ran N = 2..5 (IBM: N = 3, `d_q = 2`), and Wang
ran N = 3..8.

- **Reproduced:** `N` causal states, the "topological complexity N" of Yang. The
  phaseless quantum states are linearly independent (dimension `N`), so `d_q = 2` is a
  *lossy* setting for N = 3, as Yang states.

### 2.6 R-k golden mean and Nemo [Mahoney2016; Ho2020]

**4-3 golden mean** (Mahoney Fig. 2). State A emits 0 and stays (`p`), or emits 1 and
enters a chain that emits four 1s in total, then three 0s, and returns to A. That makes
7 states, Markov order 4 and cryptic order 3.
`rk_golden_mean(r, k, p, loop_symbol)` builds this family: `r` copies of the exit symbol,
then `k` copies of the loop symbol.

- **Reproduced:** `C_q(L)` strictly decreases up to `L = 3` and is constant afterwards,
  as the text states ("C_q(3) = C_q(L > 3)").
- **Discrepancy:** the text says "states A and G lead to A on a symbol 1", but Fig. 2
  draws G → A emitting 0. With a 1 the machine would contradict A's own 0 self-loop. We
  follow the figure.

**Ho et al.'s "3-2" golden mean** (Ho Fig. 3a) is not Mahoney's pattern with r = 3,
k = 2. Its symbols are swapped (the loop emits 1), and the exit run has length 2
followed by three loop symbols. We implement the figure literally
(`r = 2, k = 3, loop_symbol = 1`).

- **Reproduced:** `C_mu ≈ 1.435` (text; ours 1.4345) and `C_q ≈ 0.613` (Fig. 3c; ours
  0.6129). This confirms the reading. The machine's Markov order is 3, consistent with
  Ho's `R_eff = 3`.

**Nemo** (Mahoney Fig. 3): `A −0|p→ A`, `A −1|1−p→ B`, `B −1→ C`, `C −0|½→ A`,
`C −1|½→ A`. It has infinite Markov and cryptic order. Ho Fig. 4a draws the same
machine with symbols swapped and a free split `q` at C, which is the `nemo` parameter
`q`.

- **Reproduced:** `C_q(∞) ≈ 1.0332` (Mahoney text, Fig. 3 uses p = 0.666; ours 1.0336 at
  0.666 and 1.0330 at 2/3). The text does not state which `p` the number is for. Ho's
  `C_mu ≈ 1.583` at p = 0.1, q = 0.9 (ours 1.5831), and the exact-`C_q` line of Ho
  Fig. 4c at about 1.53 when read off the plot (ours 1.5205, within reading error).

### 2.7 HQMM sources: probability clock and Stern–Gerlach [Srinivasan2018]

These are generated from the quantum model with `qmem.Instrument` (`QuantumSourceTask`).

- **Probability clock** (Eq. 16): `K_1 = R(0.6) diag(0.6, 1)` and `K_2 = diag(0.8, 0)`,
  with the paper's symbols 1, 2 written as 0, 1. Outcome 1 resets the memory to `|0>`,
  so the process is a renewal process whose causal states are the counts since the last
  1. The count is unbounded, so there are countably many causal states. The quantum
  memory is ≤ 2-dimensional by construction.
- **Stern–Gerlach** (Eqs. 23–25): a spin-1/2 measured along a uniformly random axis.
  There are 6 outcomes, padded to 8. Each outcome leaves the spin in an eigenstate, so
  the process has 6 recurrent belief states (the "HMM needs 6 states" of the paper), and
  `h = log2 3 + 2/3` bits exactly.
- **Reproduced:** the true models' description accuracy, `DA = 1 − h / log2 s`. The paper
  reports test DA 0.1632 (SD 0.0111) for the clock and 0.1303 (SD 0.0047) for the spin
  model; ours are 0.1623 and 0.1290, within one SD. The paper's DA is an empirical
  average over finite sequences.
- **Assumption:** neither paper gives an initial state. We start from the outcome-averaged
  channel's fixed point, so the source is stationary (the paper discards a burn-in of
  1000 steps instead). The clock's fixed point is unique, and Stern–Gerlach's is `I/2`.

### 2.8 Synthetic 6-state / 6-symbol HMM [Adhikary2020]

**Specification status:** Adhikary et al. 2020 does not give the model. It says it reuses
"the synthetic dataset used by [Srinivasan et al. 2018]" and points to its code
repository, which we did not read. Srinivasan et al. 2018 Sec. 5.4, Eq. 26, does give a
hand-written 6-state, 6-symbol HMM (column-stochastic `A` and `C`). We implement that
model (`srinivasan_hmm`).

- **Assumptions:** (a) the model is the one Adhikary used. This is inferred from the
  citation and the matching sizes, not confirmed. (b) Moore form: emit from the current
  state, then move, so `L[y, j, i] = C[y, j] A[i, j]`. (c) The source starts from the
  stationary distribution, since neither paper gives one. (d) 6 symbols, padded to 8.
- **Supporting check:** with (b), the model's exact DA is 0.1840 (Monte Carlo entropy
  rate). Srinivasan Table 5 reports a true-model DA of 0.1838 (train) and 0.1903 (test,
  SD 0.0071). This supports both the transcription and the emission convention.
- **Exact quantities:** the source is non-unifilar, so its entropy rate has no closed
  form. The suite reports a Monte Carlo rate (200 sequences of 3000 symbols, burn-in
  1000; SE about 0.002 bits) and the exact upper bound `H(Y_t | Y_<t)` at the largest
  enumerable `t`. Per-sequence log-probabilities are exact, so model scores use the paired
  gap to the source.

## 3. Retention probes (issue #2, section 7)

### 3.1 Delayed recall at delay τ (`delayed_recall`, τ ≥ 0, tested up to 16)

Sequences are back-to-back trials of `τ + 2` binary symbols: a uniform cue bit `c`, `τ`
i.i.d. uniform distractor bits, then the **recall symbol**, which equals `c`. Every
sequence starts at a trial boundary. The recall positions are
`recall_positions(τ, T) = τ+1, 2τ+3, ...` (0-based).

- **Exact optimum:** the source is certain at the recall position (NLL 0, accuracy 1)
  and at chance elsewhere. The per-symbol optimum is therefore
  `ln 2 × (#non-recall positions)/T`, and the rate is `(τ+1)/(τ+2) ln 2`. A model with no
  memory of the cue scores `ln 2` and 0.5 at the recall position. `recall_metrics`
  reports recall-position NLL and accuracy separately from the per-symbol NLL, because
  that position is where memory shows.
- **Memory need:** information-theoretically, 1 bit (the cue) held for `τ + 1` steps.
  A time-homogeneous model must also know *when* the recall comes, which means tracking
  the trial phase (`τ + 2` values). That gives `2τ + 3` causal states and
  `C_mu = log2(τ+2) + (τ+1)/(τ+2)` bits: 5, 7, 11, 19 and 35 states for τ = 1, 2, 4, 8
  and 16. The phaseless quantum states are orthogonal (`C_q = C_mu`) because the recall
  is deterministic. **Judgement call:** we kept the clock in the task, rather than
  marking the recall position with an extra symbol, so the task stays binary and its
  optimum stays exact. Compare models on the recall-position metrics, and keep in mind
  that part of the required memory is the phase counter.
- **Noise:** `eps` (default 0) makes the recall symbol wrong with probability ε. The
  recall-position optimum is then `H(ε)` with accuracy `1 − ε`, and the rate is
  `((τ+1) ln 2 + H(ε))/(τ+2)`.

### 3.2 Noisy parity of the last k symbols (`parity`, k ≥ 1)

`y_t = y_{t−k} ⊕ … ⊕ y_{t−1} ⊕ e_t`, with `e_t ~ Bernoulli(noise)`. `noise` is the probe's ε
(default 0, a deterministic parity after the first `k` symbols; the suite uses 0.1). This is
an order-`k` Markov chain whose next symbol depends on the whole window jointly:
dropping any one context symbol leaves a fair coin (tested). There are `2^k` causal
states, `C_mu = k` bits, and the rate is `H(noise)`. The source starts from the uniform
window distribution, which is stationary for every `noise`. Phaseless `C_q` is 1.444, 2.166 and
2.888 bits for k = 2, 3, 4.

### 3.3 Mixed timescales (`mixed_timescales`)

A slow regime bit `r` flips with probability `switch_prob` per step (default 0.02, a
timescale of about 50 steps). A fast data bit `b` follows the regime's Markov rule: in
regime 0 it repeats the previous bit with probability `fast_stay` (default 0.9), and in
regime 1 it alternates with that probability. The two timescales are configured
separately.

- **Marked (default):** the symbol is `2·flipped + b` (4 symbols), so a switch is
  visible when it happens. The regime itself, the parity of all switch marks so far,
  must be held for as long as it lasts while the model processes the fast bits. The
  regime starts at 0 and the source is unifilar, with rate `H(switch_prob) + H(fast_stay)`
  exactly. There are 4 causal states and `C_mu = 2` bits: the long-lived regime bit and
  the fast last bit.
- **Hidden (`marked = false`):** the symbol is `b` only. The regime must be inferred and
  the source is non-unifilar, so only the per-sequence oracle is exact (Monte Carlo
  rate in the suite). It is included as a harder variant without an exact optimum.

### 3.4 Noise ε and the strength floor

A fixed weak measurement caps every outcome probability: `p(y|ρ) ≤ λ_max(E_y)`
(`docs/phase3d-strength.md`). An exact model of a source therefore needs operational
strength at least `η_floor = (Σ_y max_h p(y|h) − 1)/(min(Y, d) − 1)`
(`qmem.metrics.strength_lower_bound`). With ε = 0, recall and parity have deterministic
emissions, so `η_floor = 1` and only a projective measurement can be exact. That would
make any weak-measurement comparison unfair. Each probe therefore takes a noise `eps`
(default 0):

- delayed recall (`eps`): the recall symbol is wrong with probability ε;
- parity (`noise`): the output is flipped with probability ε;
- mixed timescales (`eps`): the data bit breaks its regime's rule with probability ε.

For recall and parity every emission probability then lies in `[ε, 1 − ε]`, and
`η_floor = 1 − 2ε` for any memory dimension. Mixed timescales has 4 symbols whose
probabilities are products of the switch and data factors, so its floor is already
below 1 at ε = 0 (0.80 at `d = 2`, 0.27 at `d ≥ 4` with the suite's parameters).
`benchmarks.strength_floor(task, d)` computes the floor exactly from the ε-machine's
emission rows: every causal state is reached by some history, and every other belief is
a mixture of them, so the maximum over histories is attained there.
`benchmarks.noise_profile` tabulates the exact optimum, the rate, the emission range and
`η_floor(d)` against ε. The suite summary lists ε ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2} for
d = 2, 4 and 8.

## 4. Scoring

- **Per-symbol NLL** against the exact optimum `H/T` at the evaluation length, and the
  **paired gap** model − source on the same test sequences. The gap is exact for every
  task, including non-unifilar ones.
- **Delayed recall:** recall-position NLL and accuracy (`recall_metrics`), plus the NLL
  on the other positions.
- **Description accuracy** for comparison with the HQMM literature:
  `description_accuracy(nll, s) = 1 − nll / ln s` (Srinivasan et al.'s DA for DA ≥ 0).

## 5. Memory diagnostics (`qmem.diagnostics`)

These work for any model with a quantum memory. They take an `Instrument` and an
initial memory, so they apply to architecture A (`model.instrument(), model.rho0`), the
ablations (#7) and HQMM / uMPS baselines (#6) that expose Kraus operators.
`memory_report_for_model` runs all three on a `QuantumMemoryModel`.

- **Stationary memory:** the time average of the teacher-forced conditional memory
  `rho_t` and its von Neumann entropy, for comparison with `C_q`. For an exact model fed
  stationary data, the average is `sum_j pi_j |s_j><s_j|` and its entropy is the model's
  `C_q`. This is tested on the exact perturbed-coin model, where they agree within the
  sampling error. The fixed point of the outcome-averaged channel, and the trace
  distance to it, are reported too.
- **Retention curve:** for each lag `τ`, the Holevo information between the symbol `τ`
  steps back and the current memory. This is the mutual information of the
  classical-quantum state `sum_x p(x)|x><x| ⊗ rho_x`, where `rho_x` is the average memory
  given that past symbol. Alongside it, the classical mutual information between that
  symbol and the model's next-symbol prediction (`prediction_retention`), which also
  scores the exact source, so model and optimum share one curve. Both are plug-in
  estimates, biased slightly upwards at small sample sizes. Lag 1 is the most recent
  symbol.
- **Reference-qubit purification** [Gullans2020PRL]: a reference `R` (`n_reference`
  qubits) starts in Bell pairs with the first memory qubits, and the rest of the memory
  starts in `|0>`. The memory then runs the instrument, and `S(rho_R)` is tracked. The
  outcomes are either teacher-forced data or the model's own Born-rule samples (seeded).
  The diagnostic reports the mean entropy curve, the fraction of trajectories that
  purify (below 0.01 bits), and the median purification time. Tests cover the limits: a
  projective measurement purifies in one step, and outcomes independent of the memory
  never purify.

## 6. Sanity runs (architecture A)

These use the Phase 2 recipe (2 HEA memory layers, trainable coupling, 2 joint layers,
Adam with lr 0.05, early stopping) with 5 model seeds, data seed 0, and 512/256/512
sequences of length 32. Full tables: `results/summaries/phase3c/sanity.md`. The gap is
model − source on the test set, in nats/symbol, as mean ± SE; *ok* counts seeds with a
gap below 0.01.

| task | mem qubits | gap | ok | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| perturbed coin (p = 0.3) | 1 | 0.0050 ± 0.0048 | 4/5 | median gap 0.0002 |
| perturbed coin | 2 | 0.0006 ± 0.0002 | 5/5 | |

What this shows about the suite:

- **The pipeline works end to end:** exact optima, the paired gap, recall-position
  metrics and all three diagnostics, on every sanity task.
- **Dimensional advantage reached:** on the Ghafari process, a *one-qubit* model trained
  from data reaches the optimum in every seed. Its stationary memory entropy (0.817 bits)
  matches the source's `C_q` (0.811), and its Holevo information at lag 1 is the same
  0.817 bits, below the classical requirement of 3 states (`C_mu` = 1.5 bits). The
  2-qubit model fails more often, as in Phase 2, where larger registers were harder to
  optimise.
- **Delayed recall exposes retention limits** at sizes where the other tasks are easy.
  Even at τ = 1 and 2 the model gets the recall symbol right but not *confidently*
  (recall NLL 0.2 to 0.4 nats against an optimum of 0). By τ = 8 it is at chance. Its
  Holevo information at every lag drops below 0.01 bits, and the 1-qubit reference never
  purifies. At ε = 0 the deterministic recall needs `η_floor = 1` (section 3.4), which
  the recipe's trainable but bounded coupling may not reach. The ε > 0 variants separate
  that effect from retention.

## 7. Limitations

- The phaseless `C_q` and quantum dimension are upper bounds on the minimal quantum
  memory. The PMD processes show the gap (dimension `N` versus 2). We have not searched
  for phase-optimal encodings of the other processes.
- Three sources have padded alphabets, and the padding symbols are part of what a model
  must learn.
- The 6-state HMM's identity with Adhikary et al.'s benchmark is inferred, not
  confirmed (section 2.8).
- In delayed recall, the trial-phase counter is part of the memory requirement
  (section 3.1).
